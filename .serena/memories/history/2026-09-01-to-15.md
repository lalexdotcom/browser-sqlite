| 2026-09-02 | `feat/statement-cache-byte-bound` | The statement cache's bound moves from entries to bytes, 8 MB per worker, fed by `SQLITE_STMTSTATUS_MEMUSED` read in `settle`. The rule allows one entry of overshoot and never refuses to cache, so the peak is `budget + largest statement` — and because it drops the key being re-set *before* measuring, N concurrent writers need `B > (N − 1) × MAX`, not `N × MAX`; getting that wrong would have doubled the default. Bought by measurement, not argument: a template's ceiling is ~3.4 MB and is reached by the *narrowest* table, `MEMUSED` never moves over a statement's life, and a budget too small does not degrade the cache but cancels it (+19 % Chromium, +110 % Firefox). | 2026-09-02 | `feat/vfs-default-pragmas` | Per-VFS default PRAGMAs, declared in `VFS_CAPABILITIES.defaultPragmas`, applied by `resolvePragmas` and **generated** into the README's VFS table — a default the consumer cannot see is one they cannot refuse. Exactly one VFS clears the bar of "more performance without less reliability, sourced rather than guessed": `AccessHandlePoolVFS` takes `locking_mode=exclusive` + `journal_mode=wal`, which is free there because it is single-connection by construction, and exclusive locking is what lets SQLite use its own WAL without the `xShmMap` no VFS here implements. ~4.7x on write-transaction overhead, both engines. **Consumer pragmas are merged OVER the defaults, not substituted** — the first decision was replacement, reversed when `foreign_keys` showed what it would silently cost. Three candidates were measured and rejected: universal WAL + NORMAL, `synchronous=normal`, and `cache_size` — the last one a cap rather than a reservation, costing zero bytes and buying no measurable time. | 2026-09-03 | `feat/vfs-default-pragmas` (continued) | COOPSYNC-BUSY: a read reported busy by SQLite is retried once, which closes a defect where `OPFSCoopSyncVFS` surfaced a step of its own handle-transfer protocol as a failure — one ordinary read per session, early, **on both engines and at the default `poolSize`**, not "outside Chromium" as `mem:vfs` had inferred.
